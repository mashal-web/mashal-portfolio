Building My First Website

Web Development 5 min read

I finally did it. I built my first real website from scratch. No templates, no drag-and-drop—just HTML, CSS, and a lot of googling.

The hardest part wasn't the code. It was deciding what to put on the page. Do I show my medical background? My new CS journey? Both? I realized my story is what makes me different. So I leaned into it.

Things I learned this week:

  • CSS Grid is a game-changer for layouts
  • Flexbox is great for nav bars and cards
  • JavaScript makes the site actually feel alive
  • Deploying on GitHub Pages is surprisingly easy

I'm proud of how it's turning out. It's not perfect, but it's mine. And I'm excited to keep improving it.

Why I Switched from Medical to CS

Personal Journey 4 min read

People often ask me: "Why leave medicine? You were already on that path." Here's the truth: I didn't leave medicine—I just found a different way to help.

I loved biology. I loved understanding how the body works. But I also loved logic, problem-solving, and building things. When I discovered programming, something clicked.

I realized that AI and cybersecurity could make a huge impact on healthcare—from protecting patient data to helping doctors diagnose faster. I didn't have to choose between medicine and tech. I could combine them.

So I made the switch. It was scary, but it was also the most exciting decision I've ever made. And this portfolio is the first step in that journey.

My First Week Learning JavaScript

Programming 6 min read

Week 1 of JavaScript: Confusion, frustration, and finally—understanding.

I started with variables and functions. Simple enough. Then came DOM manipulation. I remember staring at document.querySelector() for 30 minutes wondering why nothing was happening. I forgot to add an event listener. 😅

But then, something magical happened. I made a button that changed text on click. It was tiny. But it felt huge.

Here's what I've learned so far:

  • Functions are like recipes—they tell the computer what to do
  • DOM manipulation is how you make websites interactive
  • Console.log() is your best friend for debugging
  • Mistakes are normal—even experienced devs make them

Next week: arrays and objects. I'm actually excited.
